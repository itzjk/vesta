•   Non-buoyant smoke points were first observed on low-gravity aircraft.

•   Drop tower test results were ambiguous for ethylene smoke points
    owing to short development times

•   The Laminar Soot Processes Experiment (1997) with quiescent
    ambient offered the inadvertent opportunity to study smoke points in a
    quiescent ambient.

•   Two type of soot emission were observed: termed closed tip and
    open tip.

                             Conclusions


Over the presently tested range of fuel, ua, and d:
• Smoke point increases with increasing coflow velocity.

•   Smoke point decreases with increasing burner diameter.

•   Fuel sooting propensities agree with those of buoyant flames.
     – Propane < Ethylene < 50% Propylene < 75% Propylene < Propylene


•   Ethylene less sooty than propane for 0.76 mm burner. (appears
    as a different TSI for propane)

•   LSP scales with fuel mass flow rate.
     – Consistent with simplified theory.
     – Second order effect: Longer LSP for low ua / uf .
     – Coflow theory neglects radial velocity, v.
