                                          PREFACE

The National Aeronautics and Space Administration (NASA) has developed a modular, multi-
user experimentation mini-facility for conducting droplet combustion science experiments in the
microgravity environment of the International Space Station (ISS). This facility, called the
Multi-user Droplet Combustion Apparatus (MDCA), resides in Combustion Integrated Rack
(CIR), a component of the Fluids and Combustion Facility (FCF) on-board the ISS. The Flame
Extinguishment Experiment 2 (FLEX-2) uses the MDCA platform to conduct droplet
combustion tests.

This document will define the engineering and science requirements necessary to successfully
complete the FLEX-2 experiments.

1.0            INTRODUCTION

1.1            Purpose
This document will define the engineering requirements necessary to successfully complete the
science and safety requirements. This document will be used by the FLEX-2 design engineers to
develop the systems necessary for FLEX-2. Interface and carrier requirements levied on FLEX-
2 are documented in the CIR-MDCA Interface Control Document (FCF-ICD-CIR-MDCA).
FLEX-2 will use the existing Multi-User Droplet Combustion Apparatus (MDCA) hardware and
software platform already developed as a baseline for meeting many of the requirements found in
this document. Safety requirements are documented in the CIR/MDCA Integrated Hazard
Reports and the MDCA Hazard Reports (Re-Flight).


1.2            Scope
This FLEX-2 Engineering Requirements Document (ERD) serves to document two sets of
requirements: Science Requirements and Derived Engineering Requirements. The FLEX-2
Science Requirements Document (SRD) is created by the experiment’s Principle Investigators
(PI’s) and defines the requirements that are necessary to accomplish the FLEX-2 science
objectives. The purpose of this document is to provide traceability to the SRD and to formalize
these requirements. Also, it defines the engineering requirements derived to establish the
capabilities for a system that achieves the science requirements in conjunction with existing CIR
and MDCA capabilities.

3.0           MDCA OVERVIEW

3.1           Payload Description
The Multi-user Droplet Combustion Apparatus is a mini-facility designed to support Principal
Investigators (PI) interested in studying various aspects of droplet combustion in a microgravity
environment. MDCA will be integrated into the Fluids and Combustion Facility’s (FCF)
Combustion Integrated Rack (CIR) onboard the International Space Station (ISS). Currently, the
Flame Extinguishment Experiment (FLEX) and the Flame Extinguishment Experiment 2 (FLEX-
2) have been selected for use in the MDCA.


3.2           MDCA Mission
The MDCA mission is to perform microgravity experiments to study fire suppression/flame
extinguishment investigations using the CIR. The MDCA will allow investigation using pure
liquid fuels and mixtures of liquid fuels at various chamber atmosphere pressures, compositions,
and oxygen concentrations. The microgravity environment allows for measurement and
observation of flame extinguishment processes that cannot be made in sustained gravity. The
modular design of the MDCA allows for flexibility in configuring for specific experiments and
easy maintainability of the hardware. This hardware will provide the apparatus for dispensing,
deploying and igniting fuel droplets. When new droplet experiments are proposed, the additional
experiment specific hardware will be provided. This requirements document reflects such a
case, including only the requirements of the FLEX-2 investigation.


3.3           FLEX-2 Requirements Flow
Figure 1 represents the requirements flow for all of the FLEX-2 requirements. The requirements
in the Science Requirements Document (SRD) are agreed upon by the FLEX-2 Principle
Investigators (PI’s), and this document serves as the top level source of those requirements
necessary to achieve the FLEX-2 science objectives. Derived requirements come from
engineering requirements and are set by the FLEX-2 Project Team. These two sets of
requirements are incorporated into this the FLEX-2 Engineering Requirements Document (FLX-
REQ-0001).

Safety requirements are contained in the updated FLEX-2 safety review hazard reports from the
Payload Safety Review Panel (PSRP). The carrier requirements are found in the CIR/FLEX-2
Interface Control Document (FCF-ICD-CIR-MDCA).

3.4            MDCA Hardware Description
The FLEX-2 investigation will be using the existing MDCA hardware and software. MDCA
should be considered the platform on which the FLEX-2 experiments are carried out. The
MDCA hardware consists of three hardware domains: the Chamber Insert Assembly (CIA), the
Avionics Package, and MDCA Diagnostics. The CIA provides the mechanism for the
combustion event. The Avionics Package is the center of the control and power distribution for
the FLEX-2. The FLEX-2 hardware is discussed in detail below. If more detail is desired,
please refer to MDC-DOC-1364, MDCA Baseline Systems Description.


3.4.1          MDCA Chamber Insert Assembly
The MDCA Chamber Insert Assembly provides all the necessary hardware and interfaces to
perform science on one platform. Most experimental functions occur within the CIA structure
and many associated systems are found on the CIA. The CIA consists of the Internal Apparatus
(IA), which is the functional hardware and the Experiment Mounting Structure (EMS) upon
which the IA is structurally based. Other subsystems found on the CIA include the Droplet
Dispensing System, the Droplet Deployment System, the Fiber Support System, and the Ignition
System.


3.4.1.1        Experiment Mounting Structure
The EMS provides the main structure for the CIA and acts as the primary mechanical interface
with the CIR combustion chamber. It consists of two endplates, the support structure, and the
removable shrouds to protect the IA during crew activity.


3.4.1.2        Internal Apparatus
The IA provides the primary mounting platform for all experiment specific hardware and
functions such as droplet dispensing, deployment and ignition. The IA encompasses all motors
used in MDCA, as well as a cooling plate, radiometers, and some avionics hardware. The IA is
also available for future add-on hardware for different PIs.


3.4.1.2.1      Fuel Dispenser System
There are two separate fuel dispenser systems on the CIA. Each fuel dispenser system consists
of a motor housing and a replaceable reservoir. The motor housing is permanently fixed to the
IA and contains the motor, encoder, and lead screw assembly. The encoder verifies position of
the motor and allows ground operators to keep account of fuel usage. The replaceable reservoir
is composed of the syringe, the latching solenoid valve, the reservoir housing, and a transparent
Lexan cover. The replaceable reservoir assembly is located on top of the motor housing. The
syringe is covered in a Tygon sleeve and contained in aluminum housing. The syringe is
connected directly to a fuel isolation, latching, solenoid valve. The polytetraflouroethylene
(PTFE) fuel line tubing is attached to the valve with a removable fitting to disconnect the

deployment needles from the fuel system. Fuel is dispensed by moving the lead screw using a
stepper motor, which in turn pushes the plunger on the syringe.


3.4.1.2.2      Deployment System
The deployment system consists of two deployment needles made from .010-inch O.D. stainless
steel tubing supported by a ceramic sleeve. Each needle tip is flared to aid in the droplet
deployment. The needles are mounted to individual rotary servomotors with a specially designed
coupling that allows replacement of the needles and maintains the precise alignment of the
system. The motors rotate the needles to a pre-determined location where they are nearly tip-to-
tip. Metered fuel from the dispenser is routed via the PTFE tubing to one needle forming a
droplet of very precise volume. The deployment motors then actuate again, creating a slight gap
between the needles. This causes the droplet to center itself between the needle tips and
minimizes the contact area with the needles. Finally, the needles are rapidly accelerated apart,
deploying the droplet off the needle tips. The rapid acceleration leaves the droplet in place with
little or no residual motion.


3.4.1.2.3      Fiber Support System
The fiber mechanism consists of a 235 mm long, 79μm diameter silicon carbide fiber fastened
between two arms with epoxy to a tension of 15000 psi. There is a succession of small stainless
steel beads secured to the fiber at 12mm intervals. The use of the fiber mechanism allows fuel
droplets to be deployed onto, and thus tethered to, the beaded fiber at specific locations during
experimental burn and observation. The beads act to constrain the position of the droplets on the
fiber, which is especially necessary for the FLEX-2 droplet linear array experiments, where two
droplets are burned simultaneously. When used, the fiber is rotated 68o into the deployment area
using a rotary stepping gear motor. To deploy a linear array of droplets and to provide a clean
portion of the fiber for successive burns, linear translation of the fiber through the deployment
zone is achievable using a similar gear motor driving a pinion gear attached to a rack on the fiber
mechanism assembly. This translation can also be used to provide a low velocity flow over a
single burning droplet.


3.4.1.2.4      Ignition System
The ignition system uses the original MDCA configuration for single droplet ignition and for the
array experiments, modified dual droplet igniter tips are employed. The original MDCA ignition
system consists of two hot wire igniters opposed 180o, controlled by individual actuators. These
ignition wires are 4mm diameter loops of Kanthal A-1 wire, 30 AWG. These loops are mounted
into ceramic tubes and comprise the igniter tip assembly. This assembly has two pins that mate
with sockets on the igniter-mounting interface. This interface is fixed to the igniter motors,
which move the igniters into the deployment area linearly in precise steps and quickly retract the
igniters after ignition. The MDCA igniter tips are designed to be crew replaceable and for the
array experiments these will be replaced with the dual droplet igniter tips, which are based on the
original MDCA design and comply with all MDCA requirements.

3.4.1.2.5      Cooling Plate System
A cooling plate is provided to conductively cool the IA. The tubing is a stainless steel 3/8-inch
O.D. with .028-inch wall thickness. CIR compatible quick disconnect coupling fittings will
connect the cold plate to the EMS endplate. A flexible conductive lined Teflon hose with
stainless steel braiding will provide the coupling from the MDCA CIA to the CIR Interface
Resource Ring (IRR). Mating Preece connectors will be used for this interface with CIR’s IRR.
CIR’s color coding scheme will be maintained by MDCA for the inlet and outlet connectors.

The cooling system will circulate ISS cooling water flow controlled by CIR to satisfy FLEX-2
requirements. Prior to launch, the cooling system will be pre-filled to capacity with cooling
water. A gaseous nitrogen charged accumulator bottle installed inline will provide automatic
CIA protection from fluid over expansion in the system specifically during storage periods where
ambient temperatures may significantly change.


3.4.1.2.6      Radiometers
The Radiometers will provide relative measure of flame radiation and emissions through the
combustion event. The Radiometer Package consists of two Radiometers contained in a single
housing, mounted on the CIA, providing a direct view of the combustion event. The Broad-Band
Radiometer will measure total flame energy from combustion over a wavelength range covering
0.6 μm – 5.0 μm, while the Narrow-Band Radiometer will provide spectrally filtered
measurements for specific H20 emissions at 1.87 μm, using a narrow bandpass filter. Power to
the Radiometer Package is provided by MDCA Avionics.


3.4.2          MDCA Avionics
The Avionics Package provides power conditioning and power distribution, system control, data
acquisition, and operator interface for the MDCA. The Avionics Package will house the main
electronics for the MDCA hardware and will be mounted on the CIR Optics Bench.


3.4.2.1        Data and Control
A processor is responsible for automating the sequencing of operational events along with
acquiring, downlinking, and storing experiment data. Experiment control and sequencing will be
automated by the MDCA software, which is resident on the main processor. The main processor
communicates with the CIR Input/Output Processor (IOP). The interface to the CIR IOP is
necessary for transferring commands, downlinked data, and images and to receive the ISS video
and health and science (H&S) data.

3.4.2.2       Power Control and Distribution
MDCA receives its direct current, 28-Volt power from the CIR’s Electrical Power Control Unit
(EPCU) through the Avionics Package. In the Avionics Package, the power is conditioned by
filters and dc-dc converters for use by the processor, eight Axis Motor Controller, A/D and
Signal Conditioning Boards, and CIA interface board.


3.4.3         MDCA Diagnostics
MDCA Diagnostics provide acquisition and storage of all optical information concerning droplet
deployment and combustion.


3.4.3.1       Color Camera Package
The MDCA Color Camera Package will provide an overhead view of the combustion event. The
Color Camera Package consists of a Sony FCB-EX470L CCD camera, which includes a built-in
Zoom Lens with RS-232 controllable zoom and focus functions. The camera is completely
enclosed in a housing container and mounted to the CIA upper structure, internal to the chamber.
An aluminum broadband coated turning mirror is used to fold the view from the experiment to
the color camera. The Color Camera Package analog video output is converted to twisted pair,
via the Color Camera Interface Package, which then routes the output to the CIR Image
Processing and Storage Unit (IPSU-A) for viewing and storage. Power to the Color Camera
Package is supplied by MDCA Avionics.


3.4.3.2       White Light Illumination
The White Light Illumination Package supports the Color Camera Package prior to and
following the experiment event, to provide direct overhead lighting of the droplet deployment
area during dispensing, deployment and ignition for troubleshooting purposes. The White Light
Illumination is powered off upon droplet ignition, as the experiment provides its own light. The
package consists of two clusters of nine White Light LEDs mounted on the CIA upper structure.
Power to the White Light Illumination is supplied by MDCA Avionics.

APPENDIX B             DEFINITIONS

B.1            Scope
This appendix defines special terminology used in this document.


B.2            List of Definitions
Chamber Insert Assembly (CIA) – Configured equipment placed inside the combustion
chamber to perform an experiment. Usually consists of an experiment mounting structure and an
internal apparatus.

Critical Items List (CIL) – For critical failure modes, the CIL contains specific rationale, which
justifies the retention of the critical item in the system.

Experiment Mounting Structure (EMS) – Hardware that provides the structural interface from
the combustion chamber to the internal apparatus.

Failure Modes and Effects Analysis (FMEA) – Procedure by which each credible failure mode
is analyzed (failure mode occurrence, methods of detection the failure, corrective action) to
determine the effects on the system and to classify each potential failure mode in accordance
with the severity of its effect.

Internal Apparatus (IA) – The IA provides the primary mounting platform for all experiment
specific hardware and functions such as droplet dispensing, deployment and ignition. The IA
encompasses all motors used in MDCA, as well as a cooling plate, radiometers and some
avionics hardware. The IA is also available for future add-on hardware for different PIs.

Telescience Support Center (TSC) – The NASA ground facility that provides the capability to
execute ground support operations of on-orbit ISS and Space Shuttle Payloads from the Glenn
Reseach Center (GRC).
