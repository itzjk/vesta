Summary
• Two-stage hot-cool flame burning mode transitions are demonstrated to exist for the three
fuels, and the maximum flame temperature at which transition to the second stage occurred
was universally found to be ~ 1200K.
• Hot flame characteristics of all three fuels indicate that predicted extinction phenomena are all
very similar.
• Cool flame extinction properties are significantly affected by the n-alkane molecular weight,
and the chain length of the n-alkane has significant effect on the cool flame reactivity - larger n-
alkane exhibited more robust cool flame (i.e., higher temperature) and hence smaller extinction
diameters.
• Universally, d e,CF for each fuel varies linearly with initial droplet diameter, but unlike de,HF these
relationships do not collapse to a single function.
• n-Dodecane droplet combustion reveal a strong dependence of observations on ambient
pressure. Single, two-stage hot-cool flame combustion transitions to a multi-cycle combustion
behavior at ambient pressures above one atmosphere, and eventually into a hot - “Warm” flame
transition.
