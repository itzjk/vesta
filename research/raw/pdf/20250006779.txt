Background
• NASA has historically based fire safety on prevention by limiting the materials in the pressurized
volume to materials that pass the NASA flammability test (NASA-STD-6001 Test 1)
• Planned lunar surface missions will be at higher oxygen concentrations in the crew cabin (56.5
kPa (8.2 psia) and 34% oxygen)
– This presents a substantially higher flammability risk which will be mitigated by materials controls
• The uncertainty of the impact of lunar gravity on material flammability limits has been identified
as a risk by the Human Landing System (HLS) program.
– The root of this concern lies in data from low gravity testing which showed enhanced flammability
for some materials at very low flow rates that exist in reduced gravity.
• The NASA Engineering Safety Center (NESC) assembled a team comprised of people including
the NESC, HLS, NASA GRC, NASA WSTF and academic collaborators.
• This team developed a method to establish a correction factor that could be applied to NASA Test
1 data.

3

Correction Factor Approach Rational
• Despite concerns about applicability in reduced gravity, NASA Test 1 will remain the test method
for material selection due to
– Long historical pedigree
– Impracticality of reduced gravity testing for material approval
• To address the potential for gravitational effects, a method was sought to address the potential
for enhanced flammability at low-gravity
• Partial-g testing is currently limited to low-gravity aircraft (~25 s) and the NASA-GRC Zero-G drop
tower centrifuge rig (5 s).
• The drop tower facility is more available
• A protocol was developed to conduct similar tests in partial–g (NASA-GRC centrifuge) and in 1-g
at WSTF
• Both tests were limited to 5 s to allow comparison
• Both tests were upward/concurrent flammability tests

Test Campaigns
• Both the WSTF and GRC test programs used the same ignitor
• WSTF testing upward
• GRC testing necessarily horizontal during ignition, becoming “upward” after the drop starts.
• WSTF ignition was at a reduced velocity to enhance ignition and then switched to the test flow
rate
• GRC ignition was with slightly enhanced oxygen flow over ignition area which was switched off
as the drop started
• If the sample extinguished in 5 seconds it was a “pass”

Conclusions
• Some materials (cellulose and pmma) show a clear increased flammability at reduced gravity
• Higher temperature materials (Nomex and polycarbonate) may show decreased flammability at
lunar gravity conditions
• The flammability difference between 35 cm/s and no flow may be a conservative estimate of the
impact of lunar gravity buoyancy on material flammability.
• These observations should be tested for other materials and ideally in long duration lunar gravity
testing
